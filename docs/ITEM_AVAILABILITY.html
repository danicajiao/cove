Cove · how items, producers and places fit

Who makes it, where it's sold, and how

Every item has a producer who makes it and a list of places that carry it. The one thing we still need is how the item is sold. That decides what Item Details shows.

House-made isn't one of a kind. Thump's cold brew is house-made: Thump makes it and serves it only at places Thump runs. If Thump opens a second café, the cold brew is there too. A painting is one of a kind: there's one of it, so it's at one place until it sells.

The model

Three records and one link

directory.producers

Producer

Makes things: Thump, Onyx, a soda maker, an artist. Trust signals like B Corp live here and show on everything they make. The community can start a producer's page, and the producer claims it later.

directory.places

Place

Somewhere you can go. A producer can run it (Thump runs 1, Call Your Mother 5, Onyx 8), or no producer does (Gallery 12).

catalog.items

Item

One thing a producer makes. Every item has one, even if it's the shared Unknown maker. It has one kind: Goods House-made One of a kind

catalog.availability

Availability

Links an item to a place that carries it, with that place's price, whether it's new or secondhand, and when people last saw it there.

Thump the café and Thump the producer are the same business but two records. That's what lets Thump open a second café, lets Onyx run eight, and fits places that make nothing (a shop, a gallery) and producers with no shop (an artist, a soda maker).

Why it looks like this

    Chains and menus

    Same menu at every location, or not

    Call Your Mother runs 5 delis in Denver with one menu. Other restaurants change the menu by location. Both keep one item per dish. A setting on each house-made item decides which locations have it: Every location (the default) or Only some.

    What if
    Follows the setting Listed at this deli Turned off here A stored row

    Click a cell to change it. Turning off a ✓ is what someone's "Not at this location" report does. Click an item or a deli to preview its page.

    Why it looks like this

      When people add a chain's item. Someone adds the everything bagel after one visit. Because Call Your Mother runs more than one deli, it starts as Every location, since most chains keep one menu. Someone who finds it missing taps "Not at this location", which turns it off at that deli. Once Call Your Mother claims its listing, it can set each item exactly.

      Adding an item

      Two questions pick the kind

      Whoever adds the item answers these. The answer defaults to Goods, and a producer who claims the listing can change it.

      Question 1

      Was only one ever made?

      Yes → One of a kind No → go to question 2

      Ask about the design, not the copy. A shop might have the only vintage Toto lamp around, but the lamp was made many times.

      Question 2

      Can you buy it anywhere other than the maker's own places?

      Yes, or not sure → Goods No → House-made

      House-made then gets one setting: every location (the default) or only some. See chains and menus.

      Edge cases

      A bakery that sells croissants in its shop and to Onyx: Goods, since it travels.

      An original painting and prints of it are two items: One of a kind and Goods.

      A vintage Toto lamp or 90s Levi's 501s: Goods. Being secondhand is about the copy, so it goes on each shop's row.

      Backend

      Most of this is already built

      Already in the database
      directory.places.run_by_producer_id
      Which producer runs a place. Thump → 1 place, Onyx → 8, Gallery 12 → none.
      catalog.items.producer_id
      Who makes the item. Nullable today, for brand-only and secondhand items.
      catalog.availability (item_id, place_id, listing_source)
      Which places carry an item. listing_source already tells "their own place" (producer_listed) from a stockist (place_listed).
      catalog.entity_signals
      Trust signals like B Corp, attached to a producer, place or item.
      To add
      catalog.items.kind
      goods | house_made | original
      Defaults to goods. Drives the nearby section, price line, button and community prompt.
      catalog.items.producer_id becomes required
      Every item has a producer. Brands get producer pages the community can start, and the brand column folds into them. Items with an unknown maker point at one shared Unknown maker row, which has no page and stays out of producer listings and trust scoring.
      catalog.items.at_every_location
      For house-made items; defaults to true. True means the item counts as available at every place its producer runs, worked out when read, so a new location needs no copying. False means it's only where an availability row lists it.
      catalog.availability.price_cents
      Per-place price ("$24 at Thump"). items.price_cents stays as the list price.
      catalog.availability.last_seen_at, seen_count
      Backs "Seen here by 6 people · 2 days ago" and the freshness sort.
      catalog.availability.condition, quantity, note
      new | secondhand
      What a shop's copies are like: "Secondhand · 2 available · sold as a pair". One row per item per shop, even with several sizes ("W30–W34"). It records what people see, not inventory. Secondhand sightings go stale after about 2 weeks, new ones after about 4.
      catalog.items.sold_at
      For one of a kind. Set when it sells; the item stays viewable.
      Rule: rows are exceptions for every-location items
      An every-location item has no row per place. A stored row only records an exception: is_active = false turns it off at one place, and price_cents sets that place's price.
      Rule: one place for one of a kind
      An original has at most one active availability row.

      Saving places

      Visit lists

      The main button on Item Details saves a place to a visit list. People can make as many lists as they want, like one for a trip to San Francisco.

      How it works

      • Everyone starts with one list, Want to visit.
      • With one list, the button saves straight to it. With more, it opens a picker. A list can have a city, and the list for the place's city goes first, so a San Francisco bakery suggests the San Francisco trip.
      • Saving from Item Details remembers the item, so the list says why: "Sunset Bakery · for the Country Sourdough".
      • Visit lists hold places you want to go. Favorites stay for items, and following a producer or place keeps you up to date with it.

      In cove-user

      profile.visit_lists (id, user_id, name, city, created_at)
      One row per list. city is optional and drives the suggestion.
      profile.visit_list_places (list_id, place_id, item_id, added_at)
      One row per saved place. item_id is the item it was saved from, if any.

      Where things stand

      Decisions and open questions

      1. DecidedEvery location by default

        Every Thump café serves every house item unless it's turned off at one. Chains like Call Your Mother work the same way, and "Only some" covers a location's own specials.

      2. DecidedFar places still show

        Item Details lists every place that has it. Places more than 25 mi away follow a "More than 25 mi away" divider inside the scrolling row, and long lists end in "See all". Still open: the exact cutoff, and whether people can set it themselves.

      3. DecidedVisit lists live in cove-user, as many as you want

        See visit lists above.

      4. DecidedVintage isn't one of a kind

        "One of a kind" means only one was ever made. Secondhand copies of a design made many times, like a 1996 Toto lamp or 90s Levi's 501s, are Goods: one item, and each shop's copies are a row marked secondhand. Adding the lamp at a second shop should find the existing item rather than create a new one. Still open: the exact stale window, and when an era deserves its own item ("Levi's 501, 1990s" vs today's 501).

      5. DecidedLocation-based, with no Local tag

        Onboarding asks for location services, and Cove uses that location for distances and ranking. Distances are measured from the area being browsed, which is where you are by default, so someone in Denver can plan a New York trip and save places to a visit list for it. Items, places and producers don't show a Local tag. Switch "Browsing" in the explorer to see distances change. Browsing another city needs Cove to cover it; v1 is Denver only.

      6. DecidedEvery item has a producer
        • Brands are producers. The community can start a producer's page, like DWR's for the Toto lamp, and the business claims it later. Try the lamp in the explorer.
        • Unknown makers share one fallback producer, Unknown maker, which has no page. Item Details shows "Maker unknown" as plain text. Try the hand-carved chair.
        • With no producer page, "More at" the place replaces "More from".

        Still open: whether community pages cover individual makers, who today appear only as a name until they opt in.

      7. OpenBrowsing another area

        How someone picks the area they're browsing, and whether opening a visit list for another city switches to it.

      8. OpenSeasonal and limited-time items

        A special that runs for a month could be "Only some" with an end date, or switched off when it ends. Not modeled yet.
